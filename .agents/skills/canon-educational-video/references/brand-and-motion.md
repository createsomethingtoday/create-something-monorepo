## Requested camera language / PCN educational sequence
Pans and tracking carry the story organically through coherent continuous space, like one camera-shot scene. Objects remain anchored/persistent; establish geography, settle for reading, and return context where useful. A zoom must name the additional information it makes available: otherwise unreadable relevant detail, an input/validation state, or a particular contextual fact. If nothing new becomes legible or inspectable, use translation or a hold instead. No decorative scale pumping or generic zoom transitions. One-shot feel is the direction for this requested sequence, not a universal literal-one-shot rule.

Review each camera move with start/end pose, reason, information gained, object continuity and settled hold. For a detail move, compare the relevant fact at output size before and after; record visible text/detail size where useful. Do not make the overview artificially illegible just to justify a zoom. Verify intermediate frames and human playback separately. A static diagram may still be appropriate for a different brief.

## Accepted reference and its limits
The private PCN 15s continuous-camera sample, pcn-canon-continuous-camera.mp4, is the accepted visual-direction reference. It demonstrates Canon geometry, outcome-led education, one persistent world, a pan toward a bounded change and decision context, and a return to the same changed object. Study its reasoning, not its exact duration, coordinates or shot count. The example has no zoom because nothing relevant needs magnification. Acceptance of that direction does not accept future renders, narration, actual product results or publication.

Keep project-specific audits and older-version comparisons in project notes rather than reusable production rules.

## Accepted Canon isometric treatment

For Agency and PCN explanatory work, read `docs/designs/canon-isometric/ACCEPTANCE.md` and `packages/canon/src/lib/isometric/README.md`. Micah's accepted 36-second v2 reference is identified by its exact SHA there. This is an original Canon adaptation of Anatomy, not a replacement master brand or permission to execute Anatomy scripts.

Use the shared projection and semantic masters for diagrams. Detail must explain a real relationship: source, interface, decision boundary, record, instruction or ownership. No arbitrary part-count target, invented mechanism, decorative spring, or passing check that implies unmeasured results.

Small/repeated public-page illustrations are static. Larger teaching scenes may build in meaningful layers, settle completely, hold for reading, and then carry the camera continuously to the next thought. Zoom only reveals useful detail. Attached marks move with their parent. Completed objects persist in a coherent space.

Drive geometry, motion and readout from one pure timeline model. For deterministic rendering, evaluate absolute time rather than accumulating browser frames or numerical spring state. Block camera travel until every outgoing assembly has zero pending events and meets both position and velocity tolerances. Measure readable hold after settlement, not after animation starts. Seek in scrambled order and compare output; inject a late event to verify the travel gate. A new visual cut needs its own source/render review and human playback; prior acceptance does not transfer automatically.

Provide a settled complete static composition for reduced motion and narrow layouts, respect the existing film-motion control, and suspend offscreen work. Diagrams remain illustrative unless an actual fixture receipt supports the claim. Preserve native controls and human approval boundaries.
