# Draw 0.1.1 — five-person macOS pilot

Preparation kit for five automation consultants or technical leads. This kit has not been sent or published. Each participant maps one synthetic or redacted workflow independently, then reports what helped and what blocked them.

The pilot asks whether Draw makes a small workflow easier to explain and hand off. It does not test team collaboration, phone pairing, or AI generation.

## Suggested pilot

Recruit five people who regularly explain automation handoffs. Aim for three consultants and two internal technical leads, with varied mapping-tool experience. Each spends approximately 20 minutes on the exercise and 5 minutes on feedback. Offer asynchronous participation; a live observation session is optional and must be agreed separately.

Use the same candidate and instructions for all five. Record participant IDs P1–P5, architecture/OS, time to first useful map, completion, assistance needed, and severity of each problem. Keep names and contact details outside the public repository. Do not request client data, pairing secrets, or complete app-data folders.

## Suggested success criteria

These are proposed learning thresholds, not achieved results:

- At least four of five participants install, create a map and export an editable JSON backup without live help.
- At least four of five create a readable title/body/action hierarchy and can revise it later.
- All five retain their saved work after quitting and reopening; any data loss, unexplained replacement or unrecoverable save failure stops broader distribution pending investigation.
- At least four of five say the map makes the trigger, owner, approval and exception path clear enough to explain to a colleague.
- At least three of five choose a concrete next use for Draw; collect the reason from everyone who would not use it again.

Summarize task observations separately from satisfaction. Five participants can expose usability problems; they cannot establish market demand. Finish with a short decision: continue with this scope, revise a named obstacle, or pause. Any expansion, announcement, website change or App Store submission requires a separate decision.
