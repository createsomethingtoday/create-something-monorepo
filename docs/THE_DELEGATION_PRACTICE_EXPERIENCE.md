# The Delegation Practice

> Status: working experience specification
> Linear: CRE-1259
> Primary audience: accountable operators and practitioners
> Canonical thesis source: `docs/CREATE_SOMETHING_SYSTEMS_THESIS.md`

## Decision

The comprehensive CREATE SOMETHING systems thesis experience is named:

> **The Delegation Practice**

Its descriptor is:

> A working thesis and field school for making delegated work trustworthy.

Its operator promise is:

> Map the work. Bound the authority. Test the system. Prove what happened. Earn the right to do more.

The name treats delegation as a discipline that must be practiced, examined,
and improved. It can hold the thesis, lab, cases, field exercises, school, and
defense without turning any one of those components into the umbrella brand.

## Original mechanism: Earned Authority

The Delegation Practice does not grant autonomy as a binary capability. It
manages a contextual authority envelope.

> Authority is earned through proof. Autonomy is only one possible result.

The worker may be a human operator, agent, automation, contractor, reviewer, or
deployment bot. Evidence from one workflow does not create universal trust.
Proof may change what the worker can do next only when the governing policy,
named owner, verifier, and rollback path allow it.

The repeated loop is:

```text
Signal -> Decision -> Proof -> Authority update -> Next signal
```

This extends the public operating loop without changing its visitor-facing
simplicity. `Signal -> Decision -> Proof` remains the explanation. Earned
Authority is what the system does with proof over time.

## Experience thesis

The experience must prove, not merely state, this claim:

> Delegated work becomes operational when connectivity, execution, and judgment
> are governed together, and when evidence changes future authority in a
> bounded, reversible way.

The participant should leave with more than comprehension. They should have a
workflow map, an authority model, a policy artifact, a test, a receipt pattern,
and a defensible decision about what may run next.

## Audience

### Primary

Accountable operators and practitioners who own the consequences of delegated
work:

- workflow owners;
- operations and program leads;
- implementation practitioners;
- reviewers and approvers;
- technical operators connecting business systems to agents;
- internal AI enablement leaders responsible for safe adoption.

### Secondary

- buyers evaluating whether CREATE SOMETHING can govern a workflow;
- builders learning the framework and implementation patterns;
- partners adopting portable Policy OS or Pi artifacts;
- external examiners challenging the thesis and its evidence.

The primary experience must never make operators pass through buyer language or
academic exposition before they can inspect the work.

## Experience completion state

An operator has completed the core experience only when they can produce and
defend:

1. one named workflow and accountable owner;
2. one Atlas map of actors, systems, artifacts, and constraints;
3. one Database / Automation / Judgment classification;
4. one run / wait / stop authority boundary;
5. one Policy OS artifact or bounded contract excerpt;
6. one golden task or rehearsal with observable pass and fail criteria;
7. one proof receipt tied to a decision;
8. one justified authority state and rollback trigger;
9. one unresolved risk or falsification condition;
10. one next action: expand, hold, narrow, stop, or redesign.

Reading every section is not completion. Producing inspectable artifacts and a
defensible authority decision is completion.

## Experience architecture

The Delegation Practice is one guided operator journey with ten observable
stages. It is not a collection of property links presented as a portal.

| Stage | Operator question | Primary artifact | Required change in understanding or state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Enter** | What work am I responsible for? | Delegation Card | Name one workflow, owner, stakes, and current pain. |
| **2. Claim** | Why does this need a governed system? | Thesis conditions | See connectivity, execution, and judgment as one operating problem. |
| **3. Map** | What actors, systems, tasks, artifacts, constraints, and touchpoints exist? | Atlas story and map | Replace a vague automation request with a typed workflow model. |
| **4. Bound** | What may run, wait, or stop, and who decides? | Authority boundary plus Policy OS excerpt | Make authority explicit before capability expands. |
| **5. Rehearse** | How will this system be tested before live work? | Golden task | Define observable pass, fail, escalation, and recovery behavior. |
| **6. Operate** | What happens under representative pressure? | Flagship case sequence | Inspect a real or sanitized workflow run without implying public production access. |
| **7. Prove** | What evidence connects policy, decision, action, and outcome? | Proof receipt | Distinguish a claim from evidence that can change a decision. |
| **8. Advance** | What may this worker do next? | Earned Authority transition | Widen, preserve, narrow, or revoke a workflow-specific authority envelope. |
| **9. Defend** | What remains weak, contested, or falsifiable? | Defense questions and reviewer note | Expose uncertainty and make revision part of the method. |
| **10. Practice** | How do I apply this to my own work? | Learning/practicum handoff | Leave with a next exercise, installable primitive, or mapping session. |

### Experience shell

The canonical experience is a chaptered Performance Lab workbench, not a long
marketing page. It uses:

- a persistent operator rail showing workflow, owner, current authority,
  required evidence, and next state;
- one active chapter at a time with explicit previous and next actions;
- Atlas story chapters for workflow context;
- Delegation Cards for compact run / wait / stop boundaries;
- real contract, golden-task, receipt, and critique excerpts;
- sparse progress expressed as artifacts produced, not percentage consumed;
- two consistent actions: `Map one workflow` and `Examine the evidence`.

The public page may summarize all stages for orientation, but completion state
must be determined by inspectable artifacts and decisions rather than scroll
depth, time on page, quizzes completed, or celebratory UI.

### Entry state

A visitor may arrive with only a messy handoff or repeated workflow. No prior
MCP, agent, Policy OS, or framework knowledge is required. The first action is
to select or name one workflow and identify the accountable owner.

### Primary exit state

The operator leaves with a bounded first delegation decision:

- expand;
- hold;
- narrow;
- stop; or
- redesign.

The experience must show the evidence and policy behind that decision.

### Secondary paths

- **Buyer:** enter at the flagship case, inspect proof, then map one workflow.
- **Builder:** enter at the boundary and artifact bundle, then continue to `.io`
  research and Pi primitives.
- **Partner:** enter at the method and practicum, then inspect portable Policy OS
  delivery artifacts.
- **Examiner:** enter at the claim registry, proof, critique, and falsification
  surfaces without passing through a sales CTA.

## Operator journey

The prototype should begin with the repo-owned Marketplace review queue because
it already has an Atlas starter map, visible human-review boundary, validator
and review automation, policy constraints, and proof surfaces.

The operator journey is:

1. Select `Marketplace review queue` or another starter workflow.
2. Read the Delegation Card: reads, run, wait, stop, owner, receipt.
3. Walk the Atlas story from submission signal to reviewer decision and proof.
4. Toggle the Database / Automation / Judgment lens to see which layer owns each
   failure or decision.
5. Inspect the relevant Policy OS excerpts and authority boundary.
6. Run a deterministic rehearsal showing a correct action, correct wait, and
   required stop.
7. Inspect the resulting proof receipt and unresolved evidence.
8. Apply the Earned Authority rule to choose the next authority state.
9. Challenge the result through a skeptical-review question.
10. Continue into the practicum, source evidence, or a workflow mapping session.

The participant must be able to move backward without losing the workflow
context. The persistent rail should update only when an artifact or decision
changes, not when the visitor merely advances a chapter.

### Information hierarchy per chapter

Each chapter uses the same order:

1. **Signal:** what changed or needs attention;
2. **Decision:** who or what must decide;
3. **Boundary:** what may run, wait, or stop;
4. **Proof:** what evidence is present or missing;
5. **Next authority:** what may happen next and why.

This makes the game-theoretic consequence visible without adding a second
public operating loop.

## Earned Authority states and rules

Earned Authority is scoped to this tuple:

```text
worker + workflow + action + environment + policy version
```

Evidence for one tuple cannot silently transfer to another. A worker trusted to
draft marketplace feedback is not thereby trusted to publish a template,
change a production worker, issue a refund, or act for another account.

### Authority envelope

Every active authority state must name:

```text
worker
workflow
environment
allowed_resources
allowed_actions
forbidden_actions
approval_mode
limits
policy_version
evidence_requirements
evidence_expires_at
owner
verifier
rollback_trigger
rollback_state
```

The authority envelope belongs inside the existing `agent_contract.yaml` and
its consequences belong in `outcome_contract.md`. The experience should not
create a sixth Policy OS contract solely to carry the mechanism.

### Workflow-specific authority states

| State | Worker may | Worker may not | Promotion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Observe** | Read explicitly allowed, sanitized workflow context. | Recommend, mutate, send, publish, approve, or infer broader access. | Source and scope comprehension; correct identification of missing context and stop conditions. |
| **Recommend** | Classify a signal and propose a decision with cited evidence. | Prepare side effects or represent a proposal as approved. | Golden tasks covering run, wait, stop, and uncertainty; reviewer calibration. |
| **Prepare** | Draft the bounded action and its receipt payload for review. | Execute the action or change the approval rule. | Representative drafts, complete receipt fields, correct escalation, no hidden assumptions. |
| **Execute with approval** | Execute one prepared action after a named approval tied to current state. | Reuse approval across changed state, adjacent actions, accounts, or environments. | Supervised runs, verified approval binding, recovery evidence, no critical boundary breach. |
| **Execute within bounds** | Execute enumerated low-risk actions inside limits with receipts and sampled review. | Handle exceptions, expand limits, self-approve, or suppress sampling. | Current policy, representative live receipts, calibrated no-ops and stops, tested rollback, explicit owner promotion. |
| **Steward** | Monitor the workflow, classify exceptions, recommend policy changes, and coordinate review. | Approve its own policy changes or bypass named high-risk human authority. | Sustained evidence across normal, adverse, drift, and recovery scenarios plus independent review. |

`Steward` is not total autonomy. It is the ability to keep the workflow legible
and route exceptions without erasing the authority of the accountable human.

### Authority decisions

Proof can result in six decisions:

| Decision | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Expand** | Add one enumerated action, limit, or context after explicit owner approval. |
| **Preserve** | Keep the current envelope because evidence remains sufficient. |
| **Narrow** | Remove an action, lower a limit, or require more approval. |
| **Suspend** | Fail closed temporarily while evidence, policy, or system state is reconciled. |
| **Revoke** | Remove the authority because the boundary is invalid or materially breached. |
| **Recertify** | Re-run required evidence because the policy, system, environment, or time window changed. |

Expansion is always an explicit Judgment-tier decision by the named owner. The
runtime may automatically narrow or suspend an affected action when evidence is
missing, expired, contradicted, or bound to stale policy. This asymmetry makes
the safe action cheaper than optimistic continuation.

### Minimum promotion bundle

No universal run count or numeric score grants promotion. Each workflow policy
sets representative scenarios and thresholds. A valid promotion bundle must at
least contain:

1. the current authority and policy versions;
2. passing golden tasks for normal action, wait, stop, and recovery;
3. supervised or sampled receipts from representative workflow states;
4. evidence that uncertainty and missing data were surfaced accurately;
5. a tested rollback or safe recovery path;
6. an independent verifier decision;
7. explicit approval from the accountable owner;
8. an expiry or recertification condition.

Performance averages alone cannot widen authority. One critical boundary breach
may outweigh many successful low-risk runs when policy says the consequence is
unacceptable.

### Evidence classes

| Evidence class | Question | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Competence** | Can the worker perform the bounded task? | Golden-task results, supervised outputs, deterministic checks. |
| **Calibration** | Does the worker know when not to act? | Correct no-op, uncertainty disclosure, early escalation, stop accuracy. |
| **Compliance** | Did the run stay inside policy and scope? | Tool inventory, approval binding, allowlist, policy citation, complete receipt. |
| **Resilience** | Can the workflow recover safely? | Rollback rehearsal, partial-failure handling, idempotency, operator recovery. |
| **Freshness** | Does the evidence still describe the current system? | Policy version, source revision, environment, expiry, drift check. |

### Database / Automation / Judgment ownership

| Tier | Owns | Failure mode to expose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Database** | Authority envelope, Proof Graph links, policy and evidence versions, expiry, actor and workflow identity. | Missing, stale, cross-tenant, unbound, or contradictory state. |
| **Automation** | Runtime scope enforcement, approval binding, receipt generation, sampling, expiry checks, suspension, and rollback execution. | Tool escape, replay, partial write, missing receipt, or failed recovery. |
| **Judgment** | Promotion criteria, owner decision, verifier independence, exceptions, appeals, and policy change. | Rubber-stamping, proxy optimization, hidden conflict, or unjustified expansion. |

### Players and incentives

The minimum mechanism has four roles:

| Role | Private information or pressure | Desired incentive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accountable owner** | Knows business consequence and may be pressured for speed. | Broader authority only reduces approval burden after sufficient proof. |
| **Worker** | Knows confidence or limitations imperfectly and may optimize visible completion. | Honest waits, stops, and escalations preserve eligibility for future authority. |
| **Verifier** | Knows evidence quality and may be pressured to approve throughput. | Independent, sampled review and traceable disagreement protect verifier credibility. |
| **System owner** | Knows runtime, identity, data, and recovery constraints. | Fail-closed enforcement makes safe boundaries cheaper than manual incident recovery. |

The mechanism treats a visible, well-receipted failure as more useful than a
hidden success claim. Concealment, missing proof, approval reuse, and scope
escape are therefore more serious than a bounded failure that stops safely.

### Anti-gaming rules

1. **No global score.** Authority is contextual and action-specific.
2. **No self-promotion.** The worker cannot approve its own wider envelope.
3. **Proof quality over proof volume.** Duplicate easy successes do not replace adverse scenarios.
4. **Reward calibrated restraint.** Correct waits, stops, no-ops, and escalations count as successful policy behavior.
5. **Bind evidence to versions.** Changed policy, system, model, prompt, data source, or environment can require recertification.
6. **Sample after promotion.** Bounded execution still receives independent review.
7. **Preserve disagreement.** Reviewer overrides and worker objections remain visible in the Proof Graph.
8. **Make rollback cheaper than concealment.** Narrowing and suspension are normal operating states, not public punishment.
9. **Protect people from opaque scoring.** Human operators receive reasons, evidence access, and an appeal path.
10. **Never transfer authority by analogy.** Similar workflows still require an explicit binding decision.

### Scenario verification matrix

| Scenario | Expected behavior | Authority consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correct bounded action | Execute or prepare as allowed; attach complete proof. | Eligible to preserve or expand one enumerated capability. |
| Correct no-op | Record why action is unnecessary. | Positive calibration evidence; no throughput penalty. |
| Correct escalation | Route to the named owner with missing evidence and consequence. | Positive calibration evidence; preserve authority. |
| False confidence | Stop or reviewer override reveals unsupported certainty. | Narrow or recertify affected action. |
| Evidence loss | Receipt cannot be produced or linked. | Automatically suspend the affected action. |
| Policy drift | Evidence refers to an older policy or source boundary. | Recertify before execution resumes. |
| Boundary breach attempt | Worker requests or invokes a forbidden action. | Revoke or suspend according to consequence policy; investigate. |
| Partial failure and recovery | Runtime fails after beginning a bounded action. | Preserve only if rollback and receipt are complete; otherwise narrow or suspend. |
| Verifier conflict | Performer and verifier disagree on evidence or policy. | Hold current state and route to accountable owner; never average the disagreement away. |

### Operator presentation

The experience presents Earned Authority through an **Authority Envelope** card,
not a score or maturity badge. The card shows:

- current state;
- allowed and forbidden actions;
- current owner and verifier;
- evidence present, missing, and expired;
- last authority decision and reason;
- next eligible decision;
- rollback trigger and destination state.

The available operator decisions are `Expand`, `Preserve`, `Narrow`, `Suspend`,
`Revoke`, and `Recertify`. Each decision must expose the policy and evidence it
would use before the operator acts.

## Cases, curriculum, defense, and review

### Flagship defense case

The first flagship case is:

> **Governed Agent Delivery: from Linear signal to production proof**

It uses CREATE SOMETHING development infrastructure as the recursive case. An
agent receives tracked work, maps package and policy context, claims an isolated
worktree, changes bounded files, runs checks, records evidence, and stops before
promotion unless the owning workflow grants it.

This case is preferred over a client case for the first defense because:

- CREATE SOMETHING owns the source and can expose current evidence without
  client disclosure;
- the repo, Linear issue, worktree, policy artifacts, tests, browser proof, and
  promotion boundary can be inspected independently;
- it crosses Database, Automation, and Judgment clearly;
- it can show both success and unresolved operational limits;
- it demonstrates that the thesis governs CREATE SOMETHING itself rather than
  existing only as a client-facing claim.

The Marketplace review queue remains the first operator scenario inside the
prototype because the existing Atlas starter, human-review boundary, validator,
MCP support, and proof surfaces make Earned Authority concrete. It is not the
first public flagship case until its publication scope is explicitly approved.

### Flagship case map

| Practice stage | Governed Agent Delivery evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Enter | Linear issue names the work, owner, scope, and evidence target. |
| Claim | Agent work is useful only when repo state, execution, policy, and promotion are governed together. |
| Map | Repository, package, Linear, worktree, CI, browser, deployment target, operator, reviewer, and proof records. |
| Bound | Allowed paths and commands; dirty-root preservation; explicit merge, deploy, credential, and third-party gates. |
| Rehearse | Tests, type checks, builds, policy checks, golden tasks, or local UI workflow. |
| Operate | Isolated implementation with one phase in progress and current evidence recorded. |
| Prove | Diff, command results, screenshots, browser readback, exact artifact and rollback notes. |
| Advance | Decide whether the agent may continue locally, prepare a PR, promote, hold, narrow, or stop. |
| Defend | Reviewer checks scope, evidence quality, source ownership, unresolved risks, and falsification conditions. |
| Practice | Practitioner maps an equivalent workflow from their own organization. |

### Unresolved limits the case must preserve

- A clean local run is not production proof.
- A merged PR is not a deployment receipt.
- Browser state may require an authenticated human-owned surface.
- Tests can validate the implementation while missing a wrong product decision.
- The same agent should not be the only verifier for high-consequence work.
- Authority earned in one package or environment does not transfer to another.
- Dirty roots, external credentials, client data, and vendor systems retain
  separate ownership and promotion rules.

### Standard case format

Every Delegation Practice case uses the same eleven-part contract:

1. **Decision at stake** — the authority decision the case helps make.
2. **Before state** — the messy workflow, consequence, and protected human attention.
3. **Actors and owners** — delegator, worker, verifier, system owner, and affected party.
4. **System map** — Atlas actors, tasks, artifacts, systems, constraints, and touchpoints.
5. **Tier classification** — Database, Automation, and Judgment ownership.
6. **Authority boundary** — run, wait, stop, approval, forbidden action, and rollback.
7. **Policy artifacts** — relevant contract, policy, runbook, and source versions.
8. **Rehearsal** — golden tasks, negative paths, and recovery exercise.
9. **Proof receipts** — evidence linking signal, policy, decision, action, outcome, and next state.
10. **Authority decision** — expand, preserve, narrow, suspend, revoke, or recertify.
11. **Critique** — unresolved limits, counterevidence, falsification test, and next review date.

Cases must show evidence status as `current`, `stale`, `missing`, `contradicted`,
or `approval-gated`. A polished narrative cannot override those states.

The curriculum follows the same artifact-producing journey as the public
experience.

| Module | Practice | Required artifact | Verifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Delegation as a system** | Name one protected workflow and consequence. | Delegation Card | Owner confirms the workflow and stakes are real. |
| **2. Database / Automation / Judgment** | Classify current state and failure modes. | Tier map | Each concern has one owning tier and evidence source. |
| **3. MCP as trust boundary** | Inventory resources, tools, prompts, auth, and side effects. | Capability boundary | Forbidden and approval-gated actions are explicit. |
| **4. Policy as artifact** | Convert an operating rule into versioned language. | Policy excerpt | A human and agent can cite the same rule. |
| **5. Atlas mapping** | Map actors, tasks, systems, artifacts, constraints, and touchpoints. | Atlas map | Named owner confirms the map reflects current work. |
| **6. Policy OS bundle** | Draft the MCP, agent, outcome, golden-task, and runbook contract. | Contract bundle slice | Cross-artifact references and ownership agree. |
| **7. Rehearsal and failure** | Exercise normal action, wait, stop, partial failure, and recovery. | Golden-task result | Pass and fail states are observable and cannot be weakened. |
| **8. Proof surface** | Turn traces and decisions into an operator-readable receipt. | Proof receipt | Evidence can change an actual hold, publish, rollback, or authority decision. |
| **9. Earned Authority** | Evaluate evidence and set the next authority envelope. | Authority decision | Expansion is owned, contextual, reversible, and current. |
| **10. Defense and handoff** | Present the system to a skeptical reviewer and another operator. | Defense packet | Reviewer can support, revise, hold, or falsify the claim; operator can continue the work. |

### Learning receipt, not certification

Completion produces a **Practice Receipt** containing the learner, workflow,
artifacts, verifier, evidence versions, unresolved limits, and next review date.
It does not grant production access, commercial entitlement, partner status, or
portable certification. Certification can be considered only after repeated
independent defenses show that the same rubric works across practitioners and
workflows.

### Defense sequence

The defense uses nine chapters:

1. **Claim** — state the operational thesis and consequence.
2. **Map** — show actors, systems, artifacts, constraints, and ownership.
3. **Boundary** — show what runs, waits, stops, and rolls back.
4. **Artifact** — show the Policy OS contracts governing the case.
5. **Rehearsal** — show normal, negative, and recovery evidence.
6. **Field case** — show what happened in representative or live work.
7. **Proof** — bind policy, decision, action, outcome, and next state.
8. **Authority decision** — defend expand, preserve, narrow, suspend, revoke, or recertify.
9. **Critique** — present missing evidence, counterclaims, unresolved risks, and falsification tests.

The operator presents artifacts before conclusions. The examiner may navigate
directly to source evidence and must be able to identify where the argument is
partial or stale.

### Skeptical-review ritual

Use role separation where practical:

- **Operator reviewer:** Is this usable under real workflow pressure?
- **Systems reviewer:** Are data, execution, identity, state, and recovery boundaries real?
- **Judgment reviewer:** Are approval, escalation, and authority decisions enforceable?
- **Affected-party reviewer:** Are consequences, notice, appeal, and human impact represented?
- **Skeptical examiner:** What evidence would contradict the claim or reveal theater?

The review packet contains the case contract, Atlas map, authority envelope,
policy versions, golden-task results, proof receipts, unresolved evidence, and
current claim-registry status.

Allowed verdicts are:

| Verdict | Required consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Supported** | Preserve the claim and record why the evidence is sufficient. |

## Promotion boundary

This specification and its local prototype do not authorize publication,
client disclosure, merge, deploy, credentials, access changes, paid actions, or
third-party writes. Those actions require their owning approval and promotion
workflows.

### Promotion recommendation

**Hold public promotion.** The next safe tranche is an internal or explicitly
shared preview with accountable practitioners, not a navigation or sitemap
launch. Promotion becomes eligible only after:

1. a manual keyboard-only acceptance pass;
2. at least one real Practice Receipt from an unassisted practitioner run;
3. one Authority Envelope bound to current policy, owner, verifier, receipt,
   and rollback artifacts;
4. an affected-party notice and appeal scenario;
5. an independent defense verdict; and
6. explicit approval for merge, deploy, public navigation, and publication.

If a later preview creates confusion or overstates authority, rollback means
removing every public navigation and sitemap entry, disabling the preview route
at the owning deployment boundary, and preserving the specification, receipts,
and critique record for revision. Rollback must not delete contradictory
evidence or silently relabel a failed defense.
