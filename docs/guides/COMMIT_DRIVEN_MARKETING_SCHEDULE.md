# Commit-driven marketing schedule

This runbook turns verified repository work into public lessons without treating
every merge as a marketing event.

## Weekly selection

On Monday, inspect changes since the last reviewed mainline SHA. Group related
commits into one operating lesson. Use the generated Atlas/Substrate wiki to
locate the owning source and tier. Use CTX only when the reasoning or discarded
approach matters, then verify the current claim against source, tests, receipts,
or the live product.

Score each candidate from zero to two on reader relevance, evidence strength,
reusable lesson, and visual or runnable artifact. Select the strongest complete
story and keep one fallback.

The source card records the reader question, mainline source, owning files, wiki
lesson, CTX citation when used, public-safe artifact, supported and unproved
claims, channels, owner, date, and state.

Use candidate, evidence review, draft, approved, published, and outcome recorded
as separate states. Approval binds the exact content and destination.
Publishing requires its own receipt.

## Cadence

- Tuesday: LinkedIn practical lesson.
- Thursday: LinkedIn evidence; YouTube walkthrough on alternate weeks.
- Friday: technical adaptation for X and an existing clip for Instagram when
  the source supports either format.
- Up to twice each month: a validated field note through the existing CREATE
  SOMETHING newsletter and archive.

The public readback is at /dispatch. Update its queue when the editorial owner
approves a new four-week cycle. Dates reserve review capacity; they do not
authorize a post or subscriber send.

## Publication gate

1. Verify the source SHA and current behavior.
2. Confirm the artifact contains no private client data, credentials, names, or
   unapproved account details.
3. State what the evidence proves and what remains unknown.
4. Use the governed screenshot workflow when a product capture is the evidence.
5. Review the final content in its actual channel format.
6. Record the public URL, publication time, and outcome checkpoint.

Track production hours, substantive replies, artifact use where observable,
mapping conversations, qualified opportunities, and prospect-reported
influence. A qualified conversation names a workflow, accountable owner, and
plausible implementation need.
