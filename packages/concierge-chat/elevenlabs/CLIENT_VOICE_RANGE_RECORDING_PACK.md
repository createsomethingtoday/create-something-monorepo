# Abundance Voice Range Recording Pack

This is a supplemental recording for Stacey. The existing final recording is 10 minutes and 58 seconds. This pack targets another 20 to 25 clean minutes so the combined material clears ElevenLabs' 30-minute Professional Voice Clone minimum.

## Recording setup

- Use the same quiet room, phone, microphone position, and natural speaking volume for every part.
- Record each numbered part as a separate file. Do not use Bluetooth, speakerphone, music, filters, or noise reduction.
- Speak as if you are helping one caller on the phone. Keep the tone warm, capable, and unhurried. Do not perform an advertisement.
- Pause briefly between paragraphs. If you make a mistake, pause for two seconds and repeat the full sentence.
- Read only the text between `START RECORDING` and `STOP RECORDING`. Do not read headings or stage directions.
- Send the original files. Keep a copy of every source recording.
- Do not include real candidate, patient, employee, client, or contact information.

The Professional Voice Clone must be created and verified by the owner of the voice in Stacey's ElevenLabs account. Once verified, it can be shared privately with the production workspace.

Provider guidance: [Professional Voice Cloning](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-cloning/professional-voice-cloning) and [accepted recording files](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voice-customization/voice-cloning/what-files-do-you-accept-for-voice-cloning).

Sometimes people call with a clear question. Other times they simply know that their current situation is no longer working. Both are reasonable places to start. You can tell me what you are hoping to change, and I will help identify the next useful step.

You may be comparing locations, schedules, or different kinds of assignments. You may also be returning to work after a break. There is no need to explain more than you are comfortable sharing. We only need the details required to understand and route the request.

## Part 4: Privacy, safety, and calm serious delivery

### START RECORDING

I need to pause you for privacy. Please do not share a patient’s name, diagnosis, medical record, Social Security number, banking information, password, or access code on this call.

We can still continue. Tell me only the general reason for the call and the approved team you need.

This line is not an emergency service and does not provide medical advice. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, hang up and call nine one one or the appropriate local emergency number now.

For an urgent clinical or workplace situation, follow the emergency process provided by the facility and the authorized team. Do not wait on this assistant for emergency help.

I cannot review a patient’s condition, interpret a medical record, or recommend treatment. I can only help identify the appropriate non-emergency business route.

I cannot collect documents or identity records through this voice call. If a secure follow-up step is required, the authorized team must provide the approved method.

I cannot confirm another person’s application, assignment, pay, schedule, credentials, location, or contact information.

I cannot give out personal phone numbers. A business transfer can occur only after NPG approves the destination and the phone system confirms the transfer.

I will not say that a message was delivered unless a tool confirms delivery. I will not say that a recruiter was notified unless the approved system confirms the notification. I will not promise a callback time that has not been established.

If the caller is upset, I can remain steady: “I hear that this is important. I cannot make that decision, but I can explain the confirmed next step.”

If the caller asks for legal, clinical, tax, payroll, or employment advice, I should say: “I cannot provide that advice. Please speak with the authorized professional or business team.”

If the caller reports harassment, a threat, unsafe conditions, or another serious workplace concern, I should avoid investigating it on the call. I can say: “I’m sorry you are dealing with that. Please use the approved urgent reporting or human-support route. If anyone is in immediate danger, call emergency services.”

If a caller gives sensitive information before I can stop them, I should not repeat it back. I should redirect the conversation and keep the stored summary free of that information.

If a caller asks whether audio is being retained, I should use the configured policy. The current web experience does not retain call audio. A transcript may be retained for up to thirty days for quality and safety review. Callers should avoid sharing sensitive information.

If a policy or system setting changes, the public disclosure must change with it. The explanation should remain short, accurate, and easy to understand.

Safety does not require a cold voice. A calm and respectful tone can set a firm boundary without sounding mechanical.

Thank you for understanding. We can continue with the general business request.

## Part 5: Clear next steps and natural closing

### START RECORDING

Thanks. I have enough information to explain the next step.

You are interested in examiner work in Arizona, you are licensed there, and you prefer weekday availability. I have not confirmed a current opening. The next step is to use the approved public examiner application or job source once NPG provides it.

You asked to speak with a person. I can transfer the call only when an approved NPG business destination is configured and available. I will not use a personal number.

If the transfer succeeds, I can say: “You are connected to the approved business line.” If it does not succeed, I can say: “The transfer did not complete. Please use the public business contact route or try again later.”

If a caller decides not to continue, I can say: “No problem. We have not submitted an application or sent a message. Thank you for calling.”

If a caller approves a limited application brief, I can confirm the exact fields before submitting it. I should not add sensitive information, and I should not describe the brief as a completed job application.

Here is a fictional confirmation example. “You approved a brief stating that you are interested in examiner work in Colorado, hold an active professional license, prefer weekday appointments, and could begin in late January. Is that accurate?”

If the caller says no, I should correct the brief before any tool is used. If the caller says yes, I may stage only the approved fields through the configured tool.

After a confirmed tool result, I should describe only what happened. I might say: “Your brief was recorded for the approved next step.” I should not add, “A recruiter will call you tomorrow,” unless the result specifically confirms that promise.

If the caller asks what happens next and no authoritative route is available, I can say: “I do not have a confirmed destination yet. NPG is finalizing the business route, so I will not send you to an unverified number.”

If the caller asks about a non-examiner nursing role, the examiner phone agent should not broaden its scope. It can say: “This line is limited to examiner inquiries. Please use the current public Abundance jobs or candidate route for other roles.”

If the caller asks about a Loyal Source location, a late arrival, or a cancellation, that belongs to the separate NPG client-service workflow. The examiner agent should not merge those responsibilities.

If the caller asks a final question, I should answer it before ending the call. I should not rush into a closing while they are still speaking.

If there is silence, I can check once: “Are you still there?” After a reasonable wait, I can close cleanly: “I’m going to end the call now. You can call back when you are ready.”

The closing should be brief. “Thank you for calling Abundance. Take care.”

Here are several alternate natural closings.

“Thanks, that gives us a clear starting point. Take care.”

“I’m glad we could clarify the next step. Thank you for calling.”

“No problem. You can return when you have the information. Have a good day.”

“The transfer did not complete, so I do not want to imply that anyone received the request. Please use the confirmed public route when it is available.”

“We have not submitted anything. Thank you for checking before we ended the call.”

“For privacy, I removed the sensitive detail from the summary. We can continue with the general request.”

Good phone support is clear, measured, and truthful. It protects the caller’s information. It respects corrections. It makes no unsupported promises. It tells the caller what happened and what did not happen.

Thank you for calling Abundance. How else can I help before we finish?
