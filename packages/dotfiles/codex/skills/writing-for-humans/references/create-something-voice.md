# CREATE SOMETHING voice

## Personality

CREATE SOMETHING sounds like a capable systems partner: calm, precise, warm, and prepared. Ambitious about what technology can do. Honest about what has been proven. Canon brings performance and safety together: clear action, visible boundaries, and evidence you can inspect.

## Four pillars

- **Precise.** Name the problem, action, and outcome. Use concrete language. Explain technical terms when the reader needs them.
- **Capable.** Show depth through sound judgment and useful work. Make a recommendation. Put proof beside the promise.
- **Human.** Sound like a thoughtful colleague. Be direct, conversational, and respectful of the reader’s time. Warmth comes from care and clarity.
- **Accountable.** Make ownership, permissions, and limits clear. Distinguish what’s proposed, tested, deployed, and verified. Confidence follows evidence.

## Universal writing rules

- Lead with the takeaway, then explain what supports it.
- Use sentence case for headings and labels.
- Prefer active voice, concrete nouns, and strong verbs.
- Address the reader as “you” when it helps. Use “we” when CREATE SOMETHING is taking responsibility.
- Center the reader’s task and the resulting behavior.
- Give each paragraph one job. Keep it as short as the meaning allows.
- State uncertainty precisely. Say what’s known, what remains open, and how to verify it.
- Prefer periods and commas to em dashes.
- Use natural contractions. Keep humor restrained and appropriate to the stakes.
- Explain internal terms on first use. Frameworks should help the reader understand the work.
- Remove repeated conclusions, canned transitions, and claims that could describe any company.

## Avoid

These are editorial review signals, not automatic substitutions. Preserve exact quotations, code, legal language, UI labels, and technically necessary terms.

- “Simply,” “just,” and unsupported claims that something is easy.
<!-- prose-ignore-start: Teaching examples name marketing language to avoid, not product claims. -->
- “Seamless,” “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” and similar language without a concrete meaning.
<!-- prose-ignore-end -->
- Blanket claims such as “secure,” “accessible,” “production-ready,” or “fully autonomous.”
- Claiming success because a build passed. Name the behavior that was verified.
- Describing human-guided repairs as autonomous self-healing.
- Turning a bounded result into a universal promise.

## Technology and positioning

Describe CREATE SOMETHING as building the connectivity and control layer between tools and AI. Ground that description in the workflow: what connects, what can act, who has authority, and what confirms the result.

## Competition

Explain tradeoffs fairly. Focus on fragmented workflows, unclear ownership, and missing controls. Let specific capabilities and evidence establish the difference.

## Inclusive language

Write for the least-experienced credible practitioner in the field. Respect their knowledge without assuming familiarity with internal vocabulary. Avoid ableist language, gendered defaults, and exclusionary technical terms. Use descriptive link text.

## Property voice

Adapt emphasis to the property while retaining the four pillars:

- **.ltd, philosophy:** declarative and compressed. Name concepts precisely and explain them where needed.
- **.io, research:** empirical and precise. Lead with findings; include methodology and limitations appropriate to the artifact.
- **.agency, services:** confident and specific. Lead with the client’s task and supported business outcome.
- **.space, learning:** warm and practical. Show the action, expected result, and recovery when needed.

Use narrative, personal experience, or humor when the source and audience support it. Never manufacture a story or require a confessional opening.

## The Canon test

After reading, someone should understand what matters, what happens next, and what evidence supports the claim. Every sentence should help them understand, decide, or act.
