# CREATE SOMETHING voice

## Shared language standard

ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE) is the writing foundation for all CREATE SOMETHING properties and writing formats. The operator approved this direction on 2026-11-14.

Apply it to public copy, messages, reports, research, explanations, instructions, and interface text. The goal is clear language that makes tools, agents, and AI easier to understand. Marketing copy has no separate exemption.

Use the full standard as the target. This guide summarizes local application; it does not replace the official rules or dictionary. Consult [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html) and its [Issue 9 standard](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf).

### Writing and review

- Use approved words with their approved meanings and parts of speech. Review technical nouns and technical verbs under the standard's terminology rules.
- Use one name for each concept. Define unfamiliar technical terms before the reader needs them.
- Keep descriptive sentences to 25 words or fewer. Keep procedural sentences to 20 words or fewer. Apply the standard's word-count rules.
- Give each sentence one topic. Give each paragraph one topic, with no more than six sentences.
- Write instructions in the imperative. Give one instruction per sentence unless actions must occur at the same time.
- Use active voice in procedures. In descriptions, use passive voice only when the agent is unknown.
- Use the verb forms permitted by the standard. Avoid contractions, figurative language, and unnecessary synonyms.
- Put conditions before the actions they govern. Name the person or system that acts.
- For AI behavior, state the action, required approval, and failure response when relevant. Do not invent behavior that the system does not support.

### Technical vocabulary

Keep a terminology list with each project or shared domain. Each entry must record the term, meaning, permitted part of speech, canonical source, and review status. Use the same term across related artifacts.

For terms such as `AI agent`, `workflow`, and `approval`, check the actual system before writing the definition. A term in this guide is not automatically an approved STE term. Review new terms against the official terminology rules. Do not use a glossary to exempt ordinary marketing language.

### Preservation and conformance

Preserve exact quotations, code, identifiers, required interface labels, legal text, and externally controlled names. Explain them in STE where needed. Record preserved text as a review boundary; do not silently alter its meaning or claim unchecked text conforms.

Review each changed artifact against the official rules, dictionary, and applicable terminology list. Record the reviewed scope, standard issue, unresolved terms, and exceptions. Until that review is complete, describe the text as STE-targeted or STE-inspired, not ASD-STE100 compliant.

Existing content requires a separate revision and review pass. Adopting this standard does not establish conformance for existing pages or authorize publication. A passing prose checker does not establish STE conformance.

## Personality

CREATE SOMETHING sounds like a capable systems partner: calm, precise, warm, and prepared. Ambitious about what technology can do. Honest about what has been proven. Canon brings performance and safety together: clear action, visible boundaries, and evidence you can inspect.

## Four pillars

- **Precise.** Name the problem, action, and outcome. Use concrete language. Explain technical terms when the reader needs them.
- **Capable.** Show depth through sound judgment and useful work. Make a recommendation. Put proof beside the promise.
- **Human.** Sound like a thoughtful colleague. Be direct, conversational, and respectful of the reader’s time. Warmth comes from care and clarity.
- **Accountable.** Make ownership, permissions, and limits clear. Distinguish what’s proposed, tested, deployed, and verified. Confidence follows evidence.

## Universal writing rules

- Lead with the takeaway, then explain what supports it.
- Use sentence case for headings and labels.
- Prefer active voice, concrete nouns, and strong verbs.
- Address the reader as “you” when it helps. Use “we” when CREATE SOMETHING is taking responsibility.
- Center the reader’s task and the resulting behavior.
- Give each paragraph one job. Keep it as short as the meaning allows.
- State uncertainty precisely. Say what’s known, what remains open, and how to verify it.
- Prefer periods and commas to em dashes.
- Do not use contractions. Express warmth through helpful explanations and consideration for the reader.
- Explain internal terms on first use. Frameworks should help the reader understand the work.
- Remove repeated conclusions, canned transitions, and claims that could describe any company.

## Avoid

These are editorial review signals, not automatic substitutions. Preserve exact quotations, code, legal language, UI labels, and technically necessary terms.

- “Simply,” “just,” and unsupported claims that something is easy.

- “Seamless,” “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” and similar language without a concrete meaning.

- Blanket claims such as “secure,” “accessible,” “production-ready,” or “fully autonomous.”
- Claiming success because a build passed. Name the behavior that was verified.
- Describing human-guided repairs as autonomous self-healing.
- Turning a bounded result into a universal promise.

## Technology and positioning

Describe CREATE SOMETHING as building the connectivity and control layer between tools and AI. Ground that description in the workflow: what connects, what can act, who has authority, and what confirms the result.

## Competition

Explain tradeoffs fairly. Focus on fragmented workflows, unclear ownership, and missing controls. Let specific capabilities and evidence establish the difference.

## Inclusive language

Write for the least-experienced credible practitioner in the field. Respect their knowledge without assuming familiarity with internal vocabulary. Avoid ableist language, gendered defaults, and exclusionary technical terms. Use descriptive link text.

## Property voice

Adapt emphasis to the property while retaining the four pillars and the shared STE rules:

- **.ltd, philosophy:** declarative and compressed. Name concepts precisely and explain them where needed.
- **.io, research:** empirical and precise. Lead with findings; include methodology and limitations appropriate to the artifact.
- **.agency, services:** confident and specific. Lead with the client’s task and supported business outcome.
- **.space, learning:** warm and practical. Show the action, expected result, and recovery when needed.

Use factual examples and personal experience when the source and audience support them. Keep their language within the shared STE rules. Never manufacture a story.

## The Canon test

After reading, someone should understand what matters, what happens next, and what evidence supports the claim. Every sentence should help them understand, decide, or act.
