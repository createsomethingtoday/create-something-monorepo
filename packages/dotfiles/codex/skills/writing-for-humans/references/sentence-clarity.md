# Sentence Clarity

Use this reference after the document's purpose and structure are clear. Sentence repair cannot rescue a missing answer or a confused organization.

## Characters and Actions

For a difficult sentence:

1. Identify the person, team, system, or object that matters most.
2. Make that character the grammatical subject when context permits.
3. Identify the main action.
4. Express that action as a verb instead of a hidden noun.
5. Keep the subject and verb close enough that the reader does not lose the action.

Prefer:

> The team implemented the proposal and improved performance.

Over:

> The implementation of the proposal resulted in an improvement in performance.

Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, intentionally withheld, or less important than the affected object.

## Local Comprehension

At the reader's first encounter with a claim, test for a recognizable actor, meaningful action, and observable result:

1. Can the reader recognize who or what matters?
2. Can the reader name what that actor does or decides?
3. Can the reader observe the result, consequence, or changed state?

The sentence need not state every detail, but it must carry enough local meaning for the reader to continue without guessing. A later card or definition cannot rescue an unexplained metaphor, artifact-facing meta-copy, or an abstract opening.

Prefer:

> Your team sets the limits before work moves. We map the handoff, test one workflow, and keep a record of every run.

Over:

> The page now holds one argument: keep consequential judgment, make the workflow visible, then operate the first lane with proof attached.

## Information Flow

Shape sentences and paragraphs around reader expectations:

- begin with familiar information or the context established by the prior sentence
- place important new information near the end
- keep a coherent chain of topics across sentences
- avoid changing the apparent subject without a reason
- place qualifications beside the claim they limit

Individually grammatical sentences can still make a difficult paragraph when each sentence begins from a different topic.

## Clutter and Voice

After repairing structure and flow:

- remove throat-clearing and redundant qualifiers
- replace hidden verbs with actions
- cut repeated conclusions
- use familiar words when they preserve precision
- keep necessary technical vocabulary and define it locally
- vary sentence length with the thought rather than by formula
- read aloud and restore any warmth or personality lost during editing

Do not make every sentence short. Make the relationship between ideas easy to follow.

## Final Questions

- Who or what is this sentence about?
- Is that character visible near the beginning?
- What is the main action?
- Is the action expressed as a verb?
- Does the opening connect with what the reader already knows?
- Does the ending emphasize the important new information?
- Does this sentence prepare the next one?
